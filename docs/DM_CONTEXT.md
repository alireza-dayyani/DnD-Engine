# DM context assembly

`get_dm_context` calls `DmContextService` after campaign membership and optional character ownership checks. It reads authoritative campaign time/ruleset, the selected character sheet, active encounter, current or requested location, relevant NPCs, factions, relationships, active quests, facts, owned-holder knowledge, recent events and pending consequences. Source GUIDs and event IDs remain in returned records so a client can distinguish stored facts from its own interpretation.

Selection is deterministic: NPCs at the selected location sort first, then ID; other world sections sort by ID. An NPC linked to the selected mechanical character can supply a default location. Player-requested locations must be discovered. DM sees complete authorized world records before clipping; players see only Phase 6 public projections. Player knowledge is restricted to the selected owned character; an unknown secret's truth is omitted. Player events contain only metadata for that character and no event payload. The DM receives full event data, subject to clipping.

The response includes at most 10 NPCs, 5 factions, 10 relationships, 10 quests, 10 facts, 20 knowledge entries, 20 events and 10 pending consequences. Each world entry is capped at 768 serialized characters, knowledge at 512, each event or consequence at 1,024, and the encounter at 2,048. Oversized entries become `{truncated, originalChars, preview}`; `truncation` reports available/returned counts and clipped counts per section. `afterEvent` pages the event selection. This is an assembly response, not a full database export; call the specific read tools for detail.

The character sheet and small campaign header are returned intact. The player timeline scans at most 100 public events after the cursor before selecting up to 20 for the owned character, so the caller may need to advance the cursor through sparse history. Pending consequence discovery scans up to 500 events after its cursor; a DM should paginate on long campaigns. Current encounter discovery scans stored campaign encounter states so an older active encounter is not hidden by unrelated new records. Further indexed projections and token-budget controls are future work.
